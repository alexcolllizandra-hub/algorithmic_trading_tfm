## 1. The question

The gates judged each family on its **whole** out-of-sample record. That is the
right test of "does this work", but it can hide a real effect: a family could be
flat on average and still be doing something in a corner of the state space —
only when volatility is high, say, or only while the market trends.

This is scientific question **H4**. It asks one thing of the thirteen rejected
families: *is there an effect in only part of the state space?*

It is also, unavoidably, the most dangerous analysis in the thesis. Slicing a
flat record into regimes is precisely how a researcher manufactures a false
positive — with enough slices, something always looks good. The design below
exists to keep that from happening.

---

## 2. Two safeguards, and one deviation worth stating

**Safeguard 1 — every slice is causal.** A regime label computed with future
information would define the slice using knowledge the strategy could not have
had, which is leakage even when the intent is only descriptive.

**Safeguard 2 — the block is corrected within itself.** Conditioning multiplies
the tests: thirteen families across five states is 65 hypotheses, not thirteen.
Holm and Benjamini–Hochberg are applied over every cell at once. This count is
kept **separate** from the study-level count in
[study_level_multiple_testing.md](study_level_multiple_testing.md), because the
two answer different questions and merging them would misstate both.

### Where the regime labels come from

The task was framed as "wire the existing regime code in `eda/` to
`evaluation/`". That was done for the trend dimension and **deliberately not**
for volatility, for a reason worth recording.

| Dimension | Source | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Volatility (`low` / `medium` / `high`) | The `regime` column already carried by the persisted out-of-sample ledgers, from a threshold model fitted on each fold's **training** slice | Causal by construction, and already the project's modelling-stage regime detector |
| Trend (`up` / `down`) | `eda.regimes.tag_trend_volatility_regimes`, **using only its `trend_regime` output** | Price against its own trailing moving average looks strictly backwards |

`tag_trend_volatility_regimes` also produces volatility buckets, and those are
**not used here**. It computes them from full-sample quantiles, and its own
docstring says so: *"Because it is descriptive only, volatility terciles are
computed on the full sample; the modelling phase will use causal, past-only
regime features instead."*

Full-sample statistics are permitted for labelled descriptive EDA. They are not
permitted here, because of what happens next: the holdout selection rule in
Piece 3 may take a conditional candidate from this document. The moment a slice
can influence that choice, it is a decision input, and a decision input computed
with future information is leakage. The causal labels were used instead.

A truncation-invariance test pins this down: the trend labels computed on a
prefix of the sample are identical to the labels the full sample assigns to those
same bars (`tests/unit/test_regime_conditioned.py`).

### Cell sizes are not uniform across families

The volatility thresholds are fitted per fold and per run, so cells do not have
the same size for every family. Most families split roughly 61/27/13 percent
across low/medium/high; `mtf_trend_consensus`, an S1 run with its own fitted
model, splits 20/38/42. This is a property of causal, per-fold fitting, not an
error, and cell sizes are reported alongside every result.

Cells with fewer than 500 bars are reported but excluded from the corrected
family: they are too small to support a claim in either direction.

## 3. Results

65 family-by-regime cells, all large enough to test. The full table is in the
generated report; what follows is every cell that was even nominally interesting.

|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `volatility_breakout` | volatility | low | 19,652 | +41.2% | +1.01 | 0.077 | 1.000 |
| `taker_flow_extreme` | trend | up | 16,544 | +15.9% | +0.86 | 0.116 | 1.000 |
| `flow_price_divergence` | volatility | high | 4,098 | +4.3% | +1.37 | 0.124 | 1.000 |
| `volatility_breakout` | trend | up | 16,544 | +44.2% | +0.94 | 0.132 | 1.000 |
| `funding` | volatility | low | 19,652 | +17.9% | +0.54 | 0.222 | 1.000 |
| `intraday_seasonality` | trend | down | 15,841 | +15.8% | +0.54 | 0.231 | 1.000 |
| `xasset_spread_reversion` | trend | up | 16,544 | +19.3% | +0.46 | 0.261 | 1.000 |
| `funding_reversal` | volatility | medium | 8,635 | +18.8% | +0.71 | 0.274 | 1.000 |
| `momentum` | volatility | medium | 8,635 | +16.2% | +0.59 | 0.278 | 1.000 |
| `momentum` | trend | up | 16,544 | +15.9% | +0.42 | 0.304 | 1.000 |

**Surviving Holm within the block: 0 of 65. Surviving Benjamini–Hochberg: 0 of 65.**

The result is stronger than "nothing survived correction". **The best cell in the
entire analysis has a raw p-value of 0.077** — it fails a conventional threshold
*before* any correction is applied, with 65 cells' worth of selection behind it.
There was never a finding for the correction to remove.

The pattern in the table is also the one noise produces. The apparently
interesting cells are scattered across unrelated families and both dimensions,
with no mechanism connecting them; several are the mirror image of an equally
large negative cell in the same family (`volatility_breakout` is +41.2% in low
volatility and −24.6% in high; `intraday_seasonality` is +15.8% in downtrends and
−49.9% in uptrends). That is what a flat record looks like when you cut it in
half, not what a state-dependent edge looks like.

---

## 4. Conclusion

> Across 65 testable family-by-regime cells, **no cell survives multiple-testing
> correction within this exploratory block**, and none reaches nominal
> significance uncorrected. The rejected families do not conceal an effect
> confined to high or low volatility, or to trending or ranging markets.

This closes H4 negatively, and it strengthens the study-level result rather than
merely repeating it: the negative finding in
[study_level_multiple_testing.md](study_level_multiple_testing.md) is **not an
artefact of averaging across market states**. The families are not being
penalised for a good regime diluted by a bad one. They are flat everywhere.

**Consequence for the holdout.** The selection rule for Piece 3 allows a
conditional candidate from this document to be preferred, if one were better
motivated than the unconditional winner. None survived, so no conditional
candidate is offered and the rule falls through to the study-level winner. That
fall-through is the designed behaviour and must not be worked around.
